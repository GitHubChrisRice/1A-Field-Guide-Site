## Recording posture

CPD Policy 423 affirmatively recognizes lawful public recording of department members performing official duties and directs officers not to prohibit or intentionally interfere with lawful recording. Penal Code § 148(g) separately provides that recording an officer, by itself, does not constitute § 148(a) obstruction and cannot by itself supply reasonable suspicion or probable cause when the statute's location conditions are met.

The Registry therefore treats **recording a public-facing officer from a place where the recorder has the right to be, without interference, as strongly protected activity**. It does **not** extend that conclusion to deliberate recording of victims, witnesses, confidential records/screens, private communications, undercover personnel, or secure operations.

No separate published Concord Police Headquarters lobby-wide ordinary-patron camera prohibition was located in the online sources reviewed.

### 2026 comparative check — Reyes / Massimino

The recent New York/Second Circuit cases do not change the California forum classification, but they sharpen the analysis.

- *Massimino v. Benoit*, No. 25-1104 (2d Cir. Aug. 17, 2026), is persuasive only in California. It supports the distinction between **physical access** and recording what is publicly visible from a lawful sidewalk vantage. It does not make protected recording a shield against an independently justified detention; the court also upheld later enforcement tied to Connecticut law after Massimino refused an identification order. That identification result is state-law-specific and should not be transplanted into California.
- *Reyes v. City of New York*, 2026 NY Slip Op 03914, interprets New York recording statutes and does not decide the First Amendment or California Penal Code §148(g).
- The Second Circuit's August 6, 2026 *Reyes* disposition vacated the New York statutory/local-law-based preliminary injunction and expressly left the constitutional claim unresolved.
- *People v. Reyes*, 88 Misc.3d 134(A), is a New York facial-sufficiency comparator for a leave/no-reentry/reentry sequence, not California trespass authority.

Accordingly, the Registry keeps the lobby's California forum label unresolved while retaining the separate strong activity-specific recording analysis for public-facing officers.

## What a field visit still adds

The useful field work is now narrow: current entrance/lobby signs, counter privacy configuration, controlled-door boundaries, public-restroom limits, and the exact parking/pedestrian approach. Those facts do not prevent the federal NPF classification of the ordinary lobby.
