# Restriction Corpus SOP

The Restriction Corpus is the project layer that answers:

> **What government rules could realistically be invoked against First Amendment activity at this facility, and what is their current constitutional posture?**

It is separate from the forum classification and from field observation.

## Order of operations

The project does **not** begin with CPRA.

Use this order:

1. collect all reasonably applicable public statutes, regulations, ordinances, published agency policies, handbooks, use agreements, and official guidance;
2. identify current physical/posted mandates during field verification where needed;
3. map each rule to exact subareas and activities;
4. run the Constitution First restriction audit;
5. identify specific gaps in authority, implementation, training, or enforcement history;
6. use CPRA only for those gaps that cannot be answered from public sources.

This avoids broad fishing requests while preserving CPRA for records that actually matter.

## Inclusion threshold

Include a rule if it is reasonably capable of affecting:

- access or remaining in the facility;
- speech/conversation;
- signs/display;
- leafleting/literature;
- petitioning;
- assembly/demonstration;
- solicitation;
- photography/video/audio recording;
- public comment;
- tabling/physical setup;
- expressive clothing;
- press/information gathering;
- privacy/confidentiality claims;
- removal, suspension, exclusion, citation, detention, or arrest.

Do not dump every statute that could theoretically apply to any human conduct.

## Rule record

Each corpus entry should identify:

- rule title and exact source;
- source type;
- current/effective date if known;
- affected subareas;
- affected activities;
- asserted government interest;
- constitutional test;
- current project status;
- enforcement hook;
- open questions;
- supporting authorities.

## Public-source status does not equal legal validity

`RULE_LOCATED_UNTESTED` means only that the government rule exists.

It must not be displayed as `PROHIBITED` until the constitutional analysis supports that conclusion.

## When CPRA becomes necessary

Use CPRA when one of these remains material after the public-source sweep:

- unpublished internal visitor/conduct directive;
- camera/recording instruction not found publicly;
- staff training interpreting a public rule;
- sign authorization/template;
- internal instruction on removal, exclusion, trespass, police referral, or identification demands;
- records showing how a rule has actually been enforced;
- complaints, incidents, exclusions, arrests, or body-camera/CAD records tied to the rule;
- communications explaining the asserted privacy/security justification;
- policy revision history not otherwise public;
- comparator treatment relevant to viewpoint/selective enforcement;
- records showing whether a claimed rule exists at all.

The CPRA request should be targeted to the unresolved rule/question, not an undifferentiated request for “all policies.”

## Pilot standard

Before a pilot is called `RESTRICTION-CORPUS-HARDENED`, it should have:

1. current public-source corpus complete to the reasonable-applicability threshold;
2. constitutional status assigned to every material rule;
3. enforcement hook identified or marked unresolved;
4. physical-signage gap identified separately;
5. targeted CPRA list created;
6. CPRA issued only where the remaining gap could materially change the field conclusion or expose unsupported enforcement.
