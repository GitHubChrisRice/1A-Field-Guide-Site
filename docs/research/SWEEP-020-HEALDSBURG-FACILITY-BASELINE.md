## Narrow documentary forum conclusions

- Ordinary municipal sidewalks receive the traditional-forum baseline once the actual street-side strip is distinguished from a facility approach. No unmapped paved area is assigned that result.
- Police and City Hall public-service lobbies are federal NPF project inferences at medium confidence from their documented service purpose. A broader intentional opening could change the classification. Separate California labels remain open; NPF does not itself justify a recording restriction.
- The library-purpose interior fits the existing federal LPF analysis. Its meeting-room terms and California interior classification remain open.
- A council meeting opened to public participation is a federal LPF for that defined channel. Brown Act attendance, recording, comment, and removal rules are addressed in the existing [public-meetings chapter](../california/public-meetings.md). The chamber outside a covered open meeting is not classified by analogy alone.
- The City’s description of the Plaza as a public park with walking, picnics, and community gatherings supports the ordinary-park TPF baseline. A performance permit is an activity restriction to test, not automatic reclassification of the park.

These are project applications of the qualified framework, not site-specific judicial holdings. Missing signs alone do not make every documentary classification unresolved; missing facts are retained where they could change the answer.

The existing California public-forum, public-library, indoor-common-space, and public-meeting chapters own the controlling framework and its limits. Primary references for the added park and meeting citations are Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association, 460 U.S. 37, 45–46 (1983), decided February 23, 1983 (binding federal Supreme Court forum framework; does not adjudicate this Plaza), and Norse v. City of Santa Cruz, 629 F.3d 966 (9th Cir. 2010) (en banc), decided December 15, 2010 (binding Ninth Circuit meeting-channel analysis; does not designate this room for all-hours expression). ## What field verification can answer

A peaceful documentary visit can verify posted hours, public entrances, signs, counters, room access, visible control transitions, ordinary public circulation, and current complaint/meeting information. Record observations with date and defined location. A visit cannot by itself establish title, deeded right-of-way, policy history, or constitutional validity.

Each facility record separates those field items from legal and records gaps. Complete property/ROW work where a few feet could change the vantage or rule. Use targeted records requests for unpublished directives, control documents, incorporated terms, and implementation; avoid requesting a duplicate of the publicly available manual or policy.

No event history or rights-risk clearance is asserted in this baseline. Absence of an enforcement-history field means it has not been established for publication, not that adverse events are known not to exist.
