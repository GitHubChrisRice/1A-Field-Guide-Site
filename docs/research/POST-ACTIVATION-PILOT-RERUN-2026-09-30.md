# Post-Activation Contra Costa Pilot Registry Rerun

California activated on September 30, 2026. This review reruns the four existing Contra Costa pilot facilities against the qualified statewide overlay without treating activation as a reason to force facility-specific uncertainty into a resolved classification.

Pilots:
- Concord Library
- Walnut Creek Library
- Concord DMV Field Office
- Concord Police Headquarters

## Current comparative cases

The review also considers four 2026 non-California decisions.

- **Reyes v. City of New York, 2026 NY Slip Op 03914 (N.Y. June 23, 2026):** the New York Court of Appeals held that New York's state and city Right to Record statutes do not themselves extend into publicly accessible police-station lobbies. This is New York statutory interpretation, not a First Amendment holding and not California law.
- **Reyes v. City of New York, No. 23-7640 (2d Cir. Aug. 6, 2026):** the Second Circuit vacated the preliminary injunction because its New York statutory basis failed; the court expressly did not decide the pending First Amendment claim.
- **People v. Reyes, 88 Misc.3d 134(A), 2026 NY Slip Op 50675(U):** a New York facial-sufficiency trespass comparator involving an order to leave, a no-reentry warning, and later reentry. It does not define California trespass.
- **Massimino v. Benoit, No. 25-1104 (2d Cir. Aug. 17, 2026):** published Second Circuit authority recognizing a First Amendment right to record law-enforcement activity in public and, on its facts, publicly visible portions of a police station from a public sidewalk. It is persuasive only in California.

## Rerun results

### Concord Library

Retain the California `UNRESOLVED` classifications for the ordinary interior, meeting room, and Self-Service Sunday interior. The qualified statewide overlay supplies the governing analytical framework, but it does not create a close California appellate classification for those particular interior channels.

The generic California trespass/public-agency-obstruction rule is no longer a statewide research gap; facility-specific application remains fact dependent.

### Walnut Creek Library

Retain the California `UNRESOLVED` classifications for the ordinary interior, reserved rooms, study rooms, underground garage/circulation, and cafe. These are facility-specific state-constitutional or control/access questions rather than statewide qualification gaps.

### Concord DMV

Retain the California `UNRESOLVED` classifications for the facility-specific access route, customer parking, exterior queue/approach, lobby, counters, and testing area. The federal classifications remain usable. The qualified California overlay now makes removal, obstruction, recording, privacy, and enforcement analysis runnable without resolving the separate state forum label.

### Concord Police Headquarters

The recent Reyes/Massimino line is most useful here.

- Ordinary Galindo sidewalk: TPF/HIGH remains unchanged. *Massimino* reinforces, as a persuasive comparator, the distinction between physical access and recording publicly visible police-facility areas from a lawful sidewalk.
- Public lobby: federal NPF/HIGH remains unchanged; California forum classification remains `UNRESOLVED`.
- Recording public-facing officers from a lawful, noninterfering lobby vantage retains strong activity-specific support under California/Ninth Circuit/local sources.
- Victim/witness/private-report interactions remain a separate privacy analysis. The New York Court of Appeals' privacy discussion is comparative only.
- The August 2026 Second Circuit *Reyes* disposition must not be cited as a holding that a police-lobby recording ban is constitutional.
- *People v. Reyes* is useful only for sequencing: current access, validity of an exclusion, leave/no-reentry notice, later reentry, and the actual trespass elements remain distinct questions.

## County maturity

The rerun confirms that remaining pilot uncertainties are local/facility-specific rather than statewide qualification gaps.

Contra Costa County now has reconciled pilot facility records, active structured investigations, public-records work, parcel/boundary evidence, local ordinance research, and facility-specific restriction audits. That supports moving county research maturity from `Unresearched` to `Researching`, not to `Established`.
