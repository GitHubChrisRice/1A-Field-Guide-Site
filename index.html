    California 1A Auditor's Field Guide¶

    Working research preview. This is the reading layer for the California handbook while the legal research is still under active development.

    The guide is designed around a practical question: what legal rule actually applies to this place, this activity, and this government response? It avoids shortcuts such as "public property means anything goes" or "an employee told you to leave, so remaining is automatically trespass."

    Facility Registry preview¶

    The new Facility Registry applies the handbook methodology to specific public facilities. The first preview includes Concord Library, Walnut Creek Library, Concord DMV, and Concord Police Headquarters, with area-specific forum, recording, access, confidence, and field-verification notes.

    The Registry deliberately uses UNRESOLVED when the evidence does not justify a confident conclusion. Unresolved does not mean prohibited.

    Start with the situation¶

    Before using a scenario, read the Standing scenario. It defines the factual baseline so a peaceful encounter is not silently turned into an obstruction, disturbance, trespass, or disorderly-conduct hypothetical.

    Recording government activity¶

    Begin with Recording public officials, then use Public-forum doctrine when the dispute turns on the character of the place.

    An employee or officer orders you to leave¶

    Use Trespass and removal. It separates public access, facility policy, forum classification, removal authority, and the actual elements of California trespass statutes.

    Police begin questioning, detaining, frisking, or arresting you¶

    Use Police encounters for the Fourth Amendment, identification, probable cause, Fifth Amendment, Miranda, and device-search layers.

    The encounter happens at a public meeting¶

    Use Public meetings for the Brown Act and Bagley-Keene rules, plus the limited-public-forum overlay.

    You need the agency's video, records, or evidence preserved¶

    Use Public records and evidence preservation.

    You are researching a facility before a field visit¶

    Use Facility research and field verification. It traces controlling law, written policy, signage, prior incidents, exclusion/removal authority, police enforcement, and post-visit records without treating policy as law.

    One distinction that runs through the entire guide¶

    Public ownership, public access, and public-forum status are different questions.

    A government property can contain several legally different areas. An exterior public sidewalk, lobby, service counter, meeting room, employee corridor, and secured area should not be assigned one blanket constitutional label merely because they share an address.

    The guide therefore analyzes specific areas and specific activities, and it uses UNRESOLVED when the available authority does not justify a confident classification.

    Current California core¶

    The current handbook has substantive chapters for:

    • the standing factual scenario;
    • facility research and field verification methodology;
    • constitutional foundations;
    • recording public officials;
    • public-forum doctrine;
    • police encounters and identification;
    • trespass and public-agency removal;
    • disorderly conduct;
    • assemblies, dispersal orders, and emergency scenes;
    • Brown Act and Bagley-Keene meetings; and
    • public records and evidence preservation.

    Facility-specific work—such as police lobbies, DMV offices, libraries, courthouses, campuses, and other recurring locations—is intentionally being built on top of that statewide doctrinal spine rather than guessed in advance.

    Research standard¶

    Every substantive rule should be traceable to authority a reader can independently verify. The project distinguishes controlling authority from persuasive cases, agency policy, secondary commentary, and incident evidence.

    The California authority index shows the working authority map. Detailed sweep ledgers remain available through the research links in the handbook.

    Working-draft notice¶

    This guide is not legal advice. Statutes, regulations, policies, and case law change, and real encounters can turn on facts that are not present in a simplified field scenario. Check the verification date and source before relying on a rule.
