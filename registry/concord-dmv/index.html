    Indoor common-space / privacy responsibility¶

    These rows separate a facility's privacy/confidentiality duties from duties that actually apply to a visitor. A privacy interest can support a narrower restriction, but it does not become a blanket camera ban merely by being asserted.

    Read the indoor common-space doctrine.

    Area Space type Privacy context HIPAA posture Facility responsibility Visitor responsibility Recording consequence
    Ordinary lobby / waiting Waiting Area Routine Sensitive Information Possible Not Indicated DMV retains responsibility for safeguarding driver, identity, transaction, and system information under laws and policies that apply to the agency. Public-facing design does not transfer that duty to ordinary visitors. A lawful visitor does not assume DMV's confidentiality duties merely by waiting in the public lobby. Any direct restriction on the visitor must come from applicable law or a constitutionally valid rule. The possibility of personal information in the lobby can support narrower safeguards, but it does not itself establish a general no-recording rule.
    Customer service counters / transaction zones Public Service Counter Confidential Interaction Possible Not Indicated DMV remains responsible for protecting nonpublic records, screens, and transaction information from unnecessary exposure and for using appropriate operational safeguards. A member of the public does not become responsible for enforcing DMV's confidentiality duties. Independent privacy law and a valid narrowly supported facility rule can still apply to particular recording conduct. A counter-level privacy concern must be tied to the actual information or interaction at risk. That can justify a narrower restriction without establishing a lobby-wide camera ban.

    Recording posture¶

    Recording can constitute First Amendment-protected activity. No dedicated public-facing California DMV field-office ordinary-patron camera prohibition was located online. The knowledge-test phone rule is activity-specific and cannot be generalized into a branch-wide camera ban; any broader restriction requires its own legal and constitutional basis.
